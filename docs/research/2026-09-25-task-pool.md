# Task pool research for skill-placebo (2026-09-25)

Scope: choose about 40 Harbor-format candidate tasks. A pilot will then narrow them to 20 tasks with a no-skill baseline pass rate of 30–70% for:
- Claude Code on a Sonnet-class model at low or medium effort;
- OpenCode on an open-weight model.

Constraints:
- Short runs (under 10 min, under 40 turns).
- Small images that run on macOS arm64 Docker with 8 GB of RAM.
- No GPU.
- Deterministic verifiers with no internet during grading.
- General software-engineering work.

Method:
- Read-only `gh api` and HF/web reads, plus three research sub-agents whose extractions I spot-checked.
- No paid API calls. No image pulls apart from one 3.5 MB `alpine` pull to test amd64 emulation.
- Items marked **UNVERIFIED** were not confirmed from a primary source.

## TL;DR

- **Don't use SkillsBench as the main pool.** Its tasks are built to reward curated, task-specific knowledge skills:
  - 41 of 87 tasks score 0/6 for Sonnet-class models without skills.
  - Only 7 SWE tasks fall inside the target band.
  - 77 of 87 verifiers download packages at grading time.

  Keep 4 SkillsBench tasks only as a "bridge" to the JetBrains July 2026 results.
- **Recommended mix** for the 40-task pool:
  - 16 SWE-bench Verified tasks, re-calibrated against frontier-model panels and run on Epoch arm64 images;
  - 10 Terminal-Bench 2.1 tasks;
  - 10 OpenThoughts-TBLite tasks;
  - 4 SkillsBench bridge tasks.

  After the pilot, target roughly 8 SWE-bench, 5 TB2.1, 5 TBLite and 2 SkillsBench tasks.
- **Calibration risk.** The target model, `claude-sonnet-5`, is much stronger than the models with public per-task data. It scores 74.6% on TB2.1 at high effort, and 340 of 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks are solved by at least 5 of 6 frontier runs. Candidates were therefore chosen so that 2–4 of 6 frontier runs pass, not by older Sonnet rates.
- **Cost per trial:**

  | Setup | Central estimate | Range |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sonnet 5 via Claude Code, low effort | ~$0.40 | $0.25–0.60 |
  | Sonnet 5 via Claude Code, medium effort | 1.5–2× low | — |
  | Open model via OpenCode | ~$0.10 | $0.01–0.30, depending on the model |

  The full plan (pilot plus 2 × 300 main trials) comes to about **$230 central**, with a pessimistic case above $300. It needs turn and time caps and a cheap open model.

### 2.2 Key findings for pre-selection

- **SkillsBench is floor-heavy.** Pooling CC + Sonnet 4.5 and OpenHands + Sonnet 4.6 (6 no-skill trials per task, strict reward = 1):
  - 41/87 tasks at 0/6 and 5 at 6/6;
  - 26 tasks in the 20–80% range, of which only 7 are software engineering.
  - Official no-skill → with-skill numbers: CC + Sonnet 4.5 16.7 → 36.2; CC + Opus 4.7 43.0 → 61.2; OpenHands + Sonnet 4.6 33.5 → 47.2; OpenHands + GLM 5.1 32.7 → 58.4 (`official.csv`).
- **TB2 is bimodal.** Per-task rates for CC + Sonnet 4.5: 38 tasks at 0%, 8 at 1–29%, **12 at 30–70%**, 15 at 71–99%, 16 at 100%. The median trial is 4.9 min (p90 25 min, median 41 agent steps); 27% of trials run longer than 10 min.
- **Claude Code harness quirk.** CC + Sonnet 4.5 scores 0% on tasks that need a **server to keep running after the agent exits** (`kv-store-grpc`, `pypi-server`, `configure-git-webserver`, `qemu-alpine-ssh`), while other agents score 80–100%. Avoid these tasks, or pilot them first. The cause is UNVERIFIED.
- **SWE-bench Verified is near the ceiling for frontier models.** Six frontier mini-SWE-agent runs:
  - Runs: Opus 4.5, Opus 4.5-high, Opus 4.6, GPT-5.2-high ×2, Gemini 3.5 Flash; each resolves 71.8–76.8% (my computation from `per_instance_details.json`).
  - Across the 500 instances: 264 are solved by 6/6 runs, 76 by 5/6, and only **66 by 2–4/6**.
  - Sonnet 5 at high effort is close to Opus 4.8 on TB2.1 (74.6% vs 78.9%). So Sonnet-4.x-calibrated picks (for example `django-11206`: Sonnet panel 2/4, frontier 6/7) would likely hit the ceiling. I **re-ranked candidates by the frontier panel, 2–4/6.**
- **JetBrains' Sonnet 5 on SkillsBench.** Mean reward was 0.326 at low effort and 0.378 at medium. That places Sonnet 5 between OpenHands + Sonnet 4.6 (33.5) and CC + Opus 4.7 (43.0) on that suite.

## 3. Fairness: is SkillsBench biased for a general-skill benchmark?

**Yes, in three ways.**

1. **The construct doesn't match.**
   - SkillsBench measures whether curated, task-specific knowledge helps. Each task ships 1–7 `SKILL.md` files written for it.
   - Tasks where no-skill runs fail are mostly knowledge-gated: 41/87 at 0/6 for Sonnet-class models, across domains like seismology, Excel/PPTX formats and CVE internals.
   - A general workflow skill (TDD, planning, brevity, "senior engineer") cannot supply that knowledge. Its effect is therefore capped near zero on about half the suite, which dilutes any real effect and biases toward "no difference".
   - SkillsBench's own data points the same way: SWE is where curated skills help least (+4.5 pp in paper v1, Table 4; +11.6 pp in v4, second-smallest, Table 3). So SWE failures there are less about knowledge and more about process, which is the thing we want to vary.
2. **Arm contamination.**
   - The paper-era images baked skills into `/root/.claude/skills`. They were removed in commit `dd40bcc` (#720, 2026-04-23), except `radar-vital-signs/environment/Dockerfile:24-30`, which still copies them.
   - Harbor's Claude Code setup copies any image-baked `~/.claude/skills` into every arm (`claude_code.py:1895-1901`), so the no-skill and placebo arms would silently receive skills.
   - Hub revision 1.0.0 predates #720 (UNVERIFIED whether it bakes skills in).
3. **Verification isn't offline.** 77/87 verifiers install packages at grading time, and scoring uses partial credit (fraction of tests passed) on several tasks. That conflicts with the "deterministic, offline, binary" requirement unless we patch each task.

**What SkillsBench does offer:** direct comparability with JetBrains' caveman, rtk and ponytail results, which used the same harness and model family. A small bridge subset keeps that link.

**TB2.1 vs SWE-style for workflow skills:**
- **SWE-bench-style tasks** (real repo, issue text, hidden FAIL_TO_PASS/PASS_TO_PASS tests) are where TDD ("write a failing test first"), planning and "verify before done" have a clear causal path to the reward:
  - failing to reproduce the bug or to run existing tests is the typical way these tasks fail;
  - the verifier is deterministic;
  - Epoch arm64 images exist, at 0.7–1.0 GB each.

  Downsides: heavy contamination (public since 2024) and near-ceiling frontier performance. That is why selection uses the frontier panel.
- **TB2.1 and TBLite** add CLI, scripting, build and feature work with small images and short runs. But TB2 mixes in many domain puzzles (chess, FEAL, DNA, CoreWars, MIPS) and is bimodal, so only about 10 tasks qualify.

**Recommendation:** a mixed pool dominated by SWE-bench-style tasks, plus TB2.1 and TBLite for task-type diversity, plus 2–4 SkillsBench tasks as a JetBrains bridge. Report the bridge separately.

Add a fresh SWE-rebench slice later as a contamination check. It is amd64-only and its Harbor adapter is not merged yet (PR #1347).

### 4e. Pilot protocol (cheap confirmation)

1. **Prepare the tasks.**
   - Vendor the 40 tasks with pinned upstream commits.
   - Patch the Dockerfiles: Epoch arm64 `FROM` for SWE-bench, and pre-install uv, pytest, `swebench==4.0.3`, `datasets` and the other verifier dependencies.
   - Set `[verifier] network_mode = "no-network"`, and confirm Docker Desktop accepts it. If it doesn't, keep the network public but assert that nothing is downloaded.
   - Cap `[agent] timeout_sec = 600`.
2. **Run the oracle on all 40 twice** (no LLM cost). Drop tasks that fail or flake.
3. **Sonnet pilot.** 3 no-skill trials each with Claude Code + Sonnet 5 at the effort planned for the main run, with `--max-turns 40`. Keep tasks scoring 1/3 or 2/3.
   - A true-50% task passes this filter with probability 0.75.
   - A true-10% or true-90% task passes with probability 0.27.
   - If fewer than 20 remain, add trials to 0/3 or 3/3 tasks whose prior evidence sits in the band.
4. **Open-model pilot.** 2 trials per task with OpenCode + the chosen open model. Drop tasks where the harness crashes, and prefer tasks that aren't 0/2 for both models.
5. **Stratify the final 20:** about 8 SWE-bench, 5 TB2.1, 5 TBLite, 2 SkillsBench.
6. **Don't reuse pilot trials in the main analysis.** Selecting on them causes regression to the mean.

### 5.2 Estimates for this pool

Pool constraints: at most 10 min, at most 40 turns, no-skill and skill arms.

**Sonnet 5 + Claude Code, low effort: ≈$0.40 per trial (range $0.25–0.60).**
- Derived by scaling the TB2.1 high-effort token profile ($0.65, 630 s) to shorter, lower-effort runs.
- Plausible per-trial mix: about 0.4–0.8M cache-read tokens ($0.08–0.16), 30–60k uncached or cache-write tokens ($0.06–0.15), and 6–15k output tokens ($0.06–0.15).
- It is consistent with JetBrains' $0.47 at low effort on the longer SkillsBench tasks.
- Medium effort costs about 1.5–2× as much: JetBrains paid $0.98 at medium vs $0.47 at low, in different experiments.

**Open model + OpenCode: ≈$0.10 per trial (range $0.01–0.30).**
- DeepSeek-V4-Flash or Qwen3-Coder are near the bottom of the range.
- GLM-5.x or Kimi K2.7 can exceed $0.30 without provider caching.

## Key risks

1. **Calibration drift.** Every per-task prior comes from older or other models and harnesses. Sonnet 5 in Claude Code at low effort is untested per task, and many candidates may hit the ceiling. That is why there are 40 candidates plus reserves.
2. **Contamination.**
   - SWE-bench Verified is public since 2024 and TB2 since 2025-10, with solutions and 52k trajectories public.
   - Memorisation reduces sensitivity to workflow skills.
   - Plan a fresh SWE-rebench slice (amd64, Rosetta) as a robustness check.
3. **Offline verification means patching tasks.** Every TB2, SWE-bench and most SkillsBench verifiers download at grading time. Document the Dockerfile diffs and gate on oracle runs.
4. **arm64.**
   - Epoch arm64 images are "best-effort, not tested".
   - Native TB2 builds via `--force-build` are UNVERIFIED.
   - Rosetta works locally, but timing tests (cancel-async-tasks, largest-eigenval) may flip under emulation.
5. **8 GB Docker VM.** Tasks use 2–4 GB each, so run with concurrency 1–2.
6. **Harness artefacts.**
   - Claude Code background-server failures (§2.2).
   - Harbor copying image-baked `~/.claude/skills` into every arm.
   - rtk failing on an old glibc.
   - Ponytail self-activating in 0 of 10 sessions. Log whether each skill actually loads in every trial.
7. **Partial-credit verifiers** (SkillsBench): binarize at reward == 1.
8. **Power.** 20 tasks × 5 trials per arm gives roughly ±14 pp confidence intervals near a 50% pass rate. Only large effects are detectable.
9. **Budget.** Medium effort or a verbose open model can push the total above $300.
10. **Rate limits.** Docker Hub returned 429 after about 60 anonymous manifest requests during this research. Run `docker login` before bulk pulls.
